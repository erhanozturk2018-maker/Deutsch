# Diagnostic Test — Where Are You Really?

> **Level:** A1 → B1 (entry test) · **Time:** about 3 hours in **two sittings** + 30 min scoring
> **Before this:** nothing. This is the first thing you do in the course.
> **You will find out:**
> - which A1 material you can skip
> - what you should review briefly
> - what needs real consolidation
> - what must be repaired first
> - where your speaking, listening, reading, writing and pronunciation are right now

---

## How this test works (read this first)

This is not an exam you pass or fail. It is a **map**. Its most important job is to separate four things that often get mixed up:

| Level of knowing | What it means | Tested in |
|---|---|---|
| **Recognition** | You can recognise the right form when you see it | Part 2, Part 5, Part 7 |
| **Controlled production** | You can produce the form when a task tells you exactly what is needed | Part 3 |
| **Guided retrieval** | You can pull words and sentences out of memory from a meaning or situation | Part 4, Part 6 (6.1–6.20) |
| **Spontaneous production** | You can speak or write freely, fast enough for a real conversation | Part 6 (6.21–6.30), Part 8, Part 9 |

Many learners at your stage **recognise** far more than they can **produce**. The gap between the two decides what you practise, so be honest. A flattering result only sends you past the practice you need.

### What you need
- A notebook or a document for your answers. Write the item number before every answer (e.g. `3.17 den`).
- Your **phone** to record audio (Parts 6, 9, 10) and a **stopwatch** or timer.
- A **text-to-speech (TTS)** tool with a German voice, for Part 7. See [How to use TTS](#how-to-use-tts-for-part-7) below.
- Optional: an AI chat app, to help rate your writing and speaking with [the rubrics](../Resources/Rubrics.md).

### Rules
1. **No dictionary, no translator, no notes, no AI help** while you answer.
2. **Don't look at an answer key until the whole part is finished.** Keys are hidden in grey "Lösung" boxes at the end of each part.
3. **Mark every guess with `?`** (e.g. `2.6 a?`). A lucky guess counts as *wrong* for routing, because you can't rely on it when you speak.
4. **Respect the time limits.** Slowness is information, not failure.
5. If a part becomes much too hard (especially B1-level tasks), stop that part and move on. That is also information.

### Plan your two sittings

| Sitting | Parts | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Sitting 1** (reading & writing on paper) | 1 Self-assessment · 2 Recognition · 3 Grammar production · 4 Vocabulary retrieval · 5 Reading | about 90 min |
| **Sitting 2** (speaking & listening) | 6 Fluency · 7 Listening · 8 Writing · 9 Speaking · 10 Pronunciation | about 85 min |
| **Scoring** | [Scoring and routing](#scoring-and-routing) → enter results in the [Progress Tracker](../Learner_Workbook/Progress_Tracker.md) | about 30 min |

Ideally do the two sittings on the same day or on two days close together, with a real break in between.

### How to use TTS for Part 7

The texts are hidden in the **Transkript** boxes. You want to *hear* them before you *read* them:

1. Read the questions first, so you know what to listen for.
