# C2: Mastery

C2 is not "C1 with harder words". At C1 you handle anything; **at C2 you handle it the way an educated native speaker would** — with precision that survives rereading, with humour and irony where they belong, and with the ability to reconstruct an argument you have only heard once. The CEFR wording is deliberately modest: *"understands with ease virtually everything heard or read"*, *"can express themselves spontaneously, very fluently and precisely, differentiating finer shades of meaning even in more complex situations."*

> **Status: geplant.** The C2 units are **not written yet**. What exists today is this stage page and the [C2 Recap](C2_Recap/00_Overview.md) — a reference built from the CEFR C2 descriptors and this course's progression. The units are built in milestone **M11** (see `Docs/03_MILESTONES.md`).
>
> **Ehrliche Warnung:** C2 is largely not teachable by a course. It is reached through **years of reading, listening, writing and being corrected** in real settings. What a course can do is name the targets, sharpen the distinctions and give you the feedback loops. That is what this stage is designed to do — nothing more, and it says so.

---

## What you'll be able to do at the end of C2

- **Understand virtually everything** you read or hear, including fast colloquial speech, dialect-coloured German, specialist text outside your field, and literary prose.
- **Summarise information from different spoken and written sources**, reconstructing arguments and accounts in a coherent presentation.
- **Express yourself spontaneously, very fluently and precisely**, differentiating finer shades of meaning even in complex situations.
- **Write complex texts** — a report, an article, a review, a literary response — in an appropriate and effective style, with a logical structure that helps the reader find what matters.
- **Use idiom and colloquialism accurately**, and be aware of connotative levels — including when *not* to use them.
- **Backtrack and restructure around a difficulty so smoothly** that the listener hardly notices.
- **Mediate at a professional level**: interpret a text, a meeting or a cultural situation for someone who cannot access it.

Eight units, project-shaped rather than lesson-shaped, plus one review and a final assessment.

| Unit | Focus |
|---|---|

| C2-U03 Fachsprachen | Reading and writing outside your own field: law, medicine, technology, administration |

| C2-U06 Nuance & Implikatur | What is meant but not said: implicature, understatement, politeness as strategy |
| C2-U07 Redigieren & Mediation | Editing someone else's German; interpreting and summarising across languages |

## What changes compared with C1

| | C1 | C2 |
|---|---|---|
| **Comprehension** | demanding texts, implicit meaning | **virtually everything**, including dialect colour, specialist and literary text |
| **Precision** | the exact word | **the finer shade**: which of three exact words, and what each implies |
| **Idiom** | recognised, used carefully | **used accurately and deliberately**, including humour and irony |
| **Repair** | visible self-correction | **invisible**: restructuring mid-sentence without the listener noticing |
| **Writing** | well-structured genre text | **effective style**: the structure helps the reader find the significant points |
| **Mediation** | explaining a text | **reconstructing arguments from several sources** into one coherent account |
| **Errors** | rare | occasional slips, immediately repaired; no systematic gaps |

## How C2 is actually reached

1. **Volume.** Read a book a month in German, across genres. Listen to material not made for learners: interviews, panel discussions, audiobooks, satire.
2. **Production with real stakes.** Write things that someone will actually read and criticise. Speak where being misunderstood costs something.
3. **Feedback that is not about grammar.** At C2 corrections concern **word choice, register and structure**. Find a reader who will say *"that word is too strong"* rather than *"that's the wrong case."*
4. **Deliberate imitation.** Take a paragraph by a good writer, copy its structure exactly with your own content. Ten of those teach more than a grammar chapter.
5. **Specialisation.** Nobody is C2 in everything. Choose two fields and go deep; keep the rest at C1.
