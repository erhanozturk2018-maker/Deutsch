# German A1 → B1: A Speaking-First Course

**Who this is for:** a learner who already knows some German (A1, part of A2), recognises a lot when reading or listening, but finds that **the words don't come out when speaking**. The course takes you from there to the **end of B1**: talking about your life, handling everyday situations and problems, telling stories, and explaining and defending your opinions.

**What makes it different:** you **produce** German far more often than you read about it. Every lesson makes you pull words and sentences out of your memory, say them against the clock, and use them to get something done. Grammar is explained clearly (in English), but always because a real task needs it.

---

## Start here

1. **Set up your tools** (20 min; see [Tools](#tools) below).
2. **Take the [Diagnostic Test](00_Curriculum/09_Diagnostic_Test.md)** (about 3 hours in two sittings). It shows what to skip, review, consolidate or repair, and it records your "before" speaking baseline.
3. **Write your route** into the [Progress Tracker](Learner_Workbook/Progress_Tracker.md).
4. **Begin A1 Fundament** on the [A1 stage page](A1/README.md), following your route.

## The course at a glance

| Stage | What you'll be able to do | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Diagnostic** | Know exactly where you stand | [09_Diagnostic_Test](00_Curriculum/09_Diagnostic_Test.md) |
| **A1 Fundament** (5 units) | Say basic things **fast and reliably**, not just recognise them | [A1](A1/README.md) |

| **B1** (12 units + reviews) | Tell detailed stories; describe anything, even without the exact word; discuss, argue, negotiate; follow simple news | [B1](B1/README.md) |

At about **7 hours a week**, the whole course takes roughly **12 months**. If you already know part of A1 well, it goes faster.

### A typical week (about 7 hours)
- 4 lessons of about 75 minutes each
- 1 speaking session (45 min) with an AI partner, a tandem partner or a tutor
- flashcards, 10–15 minutes a day
- 2 × 30 minutes of free listening or reading for fun
- **Minimum day** (when life gets busy): 15 minutes of flashcards and one short spoken recording. That keeps the chain going.

## How a lesson works

Every lesson follows roughly the same arc, so you always know where you are:

1. 🔁 **Warm-up:** recall things from last lesson, last week and last month
2. 🔍 **Discover:** a short dialogue or text; you spot the new pattern yourself
3. 📘 **Explanation:** clear, in English, with the "why"
4. ✍️🧠 **Practice and retrieval:** from controlled exercises to saying things from memory, against the clock
5. 🎭🗣️ **Task and speaking:** use German to get something done
6. ✅ **Self-check** and 🃏 **flashcards**

Answers are hidden in grey **"Lösung"** boxes. **Always try first, then open the box.** Recalling before checking is what builds memory.

## Tools

| Tool | What for | Setup |
|---|---|---|
| **Phone voice recorder** | Speaking tasks, fluency drills, Story Bank | Any recorder app. Keep a folder per stage. |
| **Text-to-speech (TTS)** with a German voice | Listening activities (the texts are in the lessons) | Google Translate (speaker icon), Microsoft Word *Read Aloud*, or an AI app in voice mode |
| **Anki** (or another flashcard app) | Daily spaced repetition | Import the files in `Resources/Anki/` as you reach each stage |
| **An AI chat app**, voice mode if possible | Role-play partner; feedback on your writing and speaking | Lessons include ready-made prompts. Paste them in. |
| A tandem partner or tutor | Real conversation (optional, but strongly recommended from A2 on) | Tandem apps, a local language café, or an online tutor every 1–2 weeks |

Recordings stay on your device; the course never needs you to upload them.
