# C1: Effective Operational Command

At B2 you could argue precisely. **C1 is where the language stops being a task**: you read a demanding text and react to its *tone*, you speak fluently without visibly searching for words, you write a structured text on a complex subject, and you move between registers the way you move between rooms. You are no longer translating a position — you are thinking in German and choosing how to say it.

> **Status: geplant.** The C1 units are **not written yet**. What exists today is this stage page and the [C1 Recap](C1_Recap/00_Overview.md) — a reference built from the CEFR C1 descriptors and this course's own progression. The units are built in milestone **M10** (see `Docs/03_MILESTONES.md`).

---

## What you'll be able to do at the end of C1

- **Understand long and demanding texts** and recognise implicit meaning: irony, understatement, evaluation hidden in word choice.
- **Follow extended speech** even when it is not clearly structured and relationships are only implied.
- **Express yourself fluently and spontaneously** without much obvious searching, and use the language flexibly for social, academic and professional purposes.
- **Produce clear, well-structured, detailed text** on complex subjects, showing controlled use of organisational patterns, connectors and cohesive devices.
- **Sustain a position in a real discussion** with specialists, including when you are interrupted or challenged.
- **Handle academic and professional genres**: an abstract, a report, a formal opinion, minutes of a meeting, a review.
- **Mediate**: explain a German text to someone who doesn't read German, and the reverse, without losing the nuance.
- **Recognise and use register deliberately** — including irony, formality, bureaucratic language and deliberate understatement.

## What changes compared with B2

| | B2 | C1 |
|---|---|---|
| **Understanding** | the content and the writer's position | **+ the implicit**: irony, hedging, what is deliberately not said |
| **Texts** | 500–900 words, commentary | **1 000–2 000 words**, essay, scholarship, literature, official prose |
| **Speaking** | structured, in a group | **flexible and spontaneous**: reformulating live, handling interruption, adjusting to the listener |
| **Writing** | essay with a plan | **genre-appropriate**: abstract, report, review, formal opinion, minutes |
| **Grammar** | compressing and marking stance | **choosing between equivalent structures for effect** |
| **Vocabulary** | collocations and register | **connotation, precision, idiom, field-specific language** |
| **Errors** | still visible | rare, and they no longer block understanding |
| **Instructions in this course** | German only | German only, at C1 level |

## Before the units exist: what to do now

1. Finish **B2** first. There is no useful shortcut from B1 into C1.
2. Use the [C1 Recap](C1_Recap/00_Overview.md) as a map. C1 is not learned from tables — it is learned from **quantity of demanding input** plus deliberate output.
3. The C1 habit, roughly daily:
   - one long article, essay or chapter — and afterwards **one paragraph in your own words**
   - one podcast or lecture of 20+ minutes without subtitles, then a 90-second spoken summary
   - a weekly written text of 300–400 words in a **named genre**, self-corrected
   - a **collocation and connotation notebook** — at C1 the difference between two synonyms is the lesson
4. Speak with people who will not simplify for you.
