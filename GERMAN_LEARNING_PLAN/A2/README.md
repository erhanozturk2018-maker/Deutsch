# A2: Talking About Your Life, Getting Things Done

In A1 you made the basics fast and reliable. A2 is where German starts to become **useful in real life**. You'll tell people what happened, talk about the people and places in your life, explain your reasons, handle the doctor, shops, travel and appointments, and plan things with others. You'll speak in **connected stretches of 30–60 seconds**, not single sentences.

---

## What you'll be able to do at the end of A2

- Tell someone about **past experiences** in 6–10 connected sentences.
- Talk about **people, likes and gifts**; say what fits or suits someone.
- Describe **your flat** and say where things are.
- Give **reasons** for your habits and opinions.
- Describe **symptoms** at the doctor's and call in sick.
- Describe and **compare products**; return something in a shop.
- **Compare travel options**, book, and deal with a delay.
- **Make and change appointments** by phone.
- **Invite** someone, accept or decline, and **plan something together**.
- Explain a **technical problem** and **give advice**.
- Write short personal and semi-formal **messages and emails**.

## How an A2 unit works

Every A2 unit is a **folder with five files**:

| File | What it is | Time |
|---|---|---|
| `00_Overview_und_Wortschatz` | The unit map: goals, the lessons, the grammar at a glance, the **word bank**. Look at it first; come back to it for words. | 10 min |
| `L1`, `L2`, `L3` | New-content lessons | 60–90 min each |
| `L4_Anwenden` | **Using it:** the unit's main task, reading, listening, writing, mixed review with older material, and a 10-minute unit quiz | 60–90 min |

**Every lesson follows the same arc:**

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 🔁 **Aufwärmen (3-2-1)** | 3 things from last lesson, 2 from about a week ago, 1–2 from a month or more ago. It's retrieval, not revision: say them before you check. |
| 🔍 **Entdecken** | A short text or dialogue first. **You** spot the pattern. |
| 📘 **Explanation** | Clear, in English, with the *why*. |
| ✍️ 🧠 **Practice and retrieval** | From guided exercises to saying things from memory, against the clock (⏱️). |
| 🎭 🗣️ **Task and speaking** | Use the German to get something done; often with a ready-made AI role-play prompt. |
| 👄 ⚠️ ✅ 🃏 | Pronunciation minute, typical mistakes, self-check, flashcards. |

**A2 vs A1:**
- There's **no Schnelltest routing** any more: A2 is new for most learners, so every lesson is for everyone. If your diagnostic showed you already know a topic, move faster through its ✍️ practice, and **never skip the speaking**.
- Instructions stay in English. Task instructions switch to German in B1.
- Speaking tasks grow from 30 to **60 seconds** and become less scripted: by the end of A2, many tasks give you only a goal.

---

## Rhythm and reviews

- **One unit ≈ 1–1.5 weeks** at about 7 hours a week: 4 lessons, a speaking session, flashcards daily, some free listening or reading.
- **Every 3 units** there's a cumulative review (R1–R3) that mixes **everything so far, including A1**: 50% recent, 30% earlier, 20% oldest. That's where your [Error Log](../Learner_Workbook/Error_Log.md) top 5 gets fixed.
- **Midpoint and Exit checkpoints** show whether A2 is really solid. The Exit decides whether you're ready for B1. Your Story Bank A2 recordings are part of it.

**Flashcards:** import `Resources/Anki/A2.tsv` into Anki. Every card is tagged with its unit and lesson, so you can study as you go.
