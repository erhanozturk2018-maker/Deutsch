# A1 Recap — Überblick

> **Level:** A1 (complete: five units + checkpoint) · **What this is:** the whole level consolidated into three reference sheets.
> **It answers one question:** *what should I know at the end of A1?*
> **It is not a lesson.** No exercises, no explanations of the "why" — those live in the units. This is what you open **after** you have learned something, to check it or revise it.

## The three sheets

| Sheet | What is in it | Wann benutzen? |
|---|---|---|
| [01 · Wortschatz](01_Wortschatz.md) | The A1 core vocabulary by topic: people, food, shopping, day and time, home, town and transport, plus the most frequent verbs, adjectives and little words | Before a speaking session; when you keep missing the same everyday word |
| [02 · Redemittel](02_Redemittel.md) | Ready-made phrases and sentence patterns by purpose: greeting, introducing yourself, asking, not understanding, requests, shopping, times, directions, reacting | Before any real conversation; 10 minutes before a role-play |
| [03 · Grammatik](03_Grammatik.md) | Every A1 structure with its forms, word order, examples and the typical mistakes: verbs, cases, negation, modals, separable verbs, imperative, prepositions, *war/hatte* | When a form is unclear; the evening before the checkpoint |

## Wie Sie damit arbeiten

1. **Cover the German column and say it.** Reading a list is not revision; retrieving it is.
2. **★ = you must be able to say it**, not only recognise it. Unmarked items: recognise them.
3. Work in **10-minute blocks**, one topic at a time. Nobody revises a whole level in one sitting.
4. Whatever you cannot produce, go back to the **unit** named in the sheet and redo that section's ⏱️ activity — not the whole unit.
5. Then take the [A1 Checkpoint](../A1_Checkpoint.md).
