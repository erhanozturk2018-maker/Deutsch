> **You will be able to:** introduce yourself fluently and get to know someone, fast and without translating in your head.

## 🎯 Ziele

By the end of this unit you can:
- **introduce yourself** in 30 seconds without long pauses: name, origin, home, job, languages, family, a hobby
- **ask at least 8 different personal questions**, and a follow-up question after every answer
- **understand and give numbers**: age, phone numbers, prices, years, and spell names
- build every sentence with the **verb in position 2**, whatever comes first

## §A — Verbs in the present tense `A1-U01-§A`

### 📘 Refresh

German verbs **show who is doing something** through their endings. You can often hear *who* from the verb alone, so the endings matter for being understood:

| | *wohnen* | *arbeiten* | *sein* | *haben* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ich | wohn**e** | arbeit**e** | **bin** | hab**e** |
| du | wohn**st** | arbeit**est** | **bist** | **hast** |
| er / sie / es | wohn**t** | arbeit**et** | **ist** | **hat** |
| wir | wohn**en** | arbeit**en** | **sind** | hab**en** |
| ihr | wohn**t** | arbeit**et** | **seid** | hab**t** |
| sie / Sie | wohn**en** | arbeit**en** | **sind** | hab**en** |

**Four things to watch:**
1. **Stems ending in -t/-d** add an **-e-** so you can pronounce them: *du arbeit**e**st, er find**e**t*.
2. **Stem-changing verbs** change their vowel, **only for *du* and *er/sie/es***:
   - e → i: *sprechen → du **sprichst**, er **spricht*** (also *essen – isst, nehmen – nimmt, helfen – hilft, geben – gibt*)
   - e → ie: *lesen → du **liest**, sie **liest*** (also *sehen – sieht*)
   - a → ä: *fahren → du **fährst**, er **fährt*** (also *schlafen – schläft*)
3. **Stems ending in -s/-ß/-z** only add **-t** for *du*: *du **heißt***, not *heißst*.
4. **One present tense for three English tenses.** *Ich lerne Deutsch* = *I learn German / I'm learning German*. *Ich fahre morgen nach Berlin* = *I'm going to Berlin tomorrow*. German has no "-ing" form; add *gerade* if you want to stress "right now".

More forms: [Grammar Tables → Present tense](../Resources/Grammar_Tables.md#a1-present-tense).

### 💬 Beispiele

First evening at a language course:
- **Ich heiße** Mira und **komme** aus Indien. → *My name is Mira and I'm from India.*
- **Wohnst** du auch in Leipzig? – Nein, ich **wohne** in Halle. → *Do you live in Leipzig too? – No, I live in Halle.*
- Mein Mann **arbeitet** bei einer Bank. → *My husband works at a bank.*
- **Sprichst** du Spanisch? – Ja, ein bisschen. → *Do you speak Spanish? – Yes, a little.* [spoken]
- **Seid** ihr auch neu hier? – Ja, wir **sind** seit Montag hier. → *Are you new here too? – Yes, we've been here since Monday.*
- Sie **fährt** jeden Tag mit dem Rad zur Arbeit. → *She cycles to work every day.*
- Ich **lerne** gerade Deutsch. → *I'm learning German at the moment.*

## §B — Verb in position 2: the Sentence Map `A1-U01-§B`

### 📘 Refresh

A German statement has one fixed seat: **the conjugated verb sits in position 2.** In front of it there is room for **exactly one element**. That can be the subject, but also a time, a place, or an object. Whatever you put first, the verb stays second, and the subject moves behind it:

| Vorfeld | Pos. 2 (Verb 1) | Mittelfeld | Satzende (Verb 2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ich | arbeite | heute zu Hause. | |
| Heute | arbeite | ich zu Hause. | |
| Zu Hause | arbeite | ich heute. | |

**Why move things to the front?** To connect to what was just said, or to set the scene: *Am Wochenende schlafe ich lange.* Starting every sentence with *ich* is correct, but sounds robotic. Germans put times and places first all the time.

> ⚠️ **The English trap.** English puts the subject before the verb, even after a time: *Today **I work** at home.* German doesn't allow two elements before the verb: ❌ *Heute ich arbeite* → ✅ *Heute **arbeite ich***.

***und, aber, oder* don't count.** They sit in front of the map: *…, **aber** heute **arbeite** ich zu Hause.* Be careful with ***dann***: it *does* count. ❌ *Und dann wir gehen* → ✅ *Und dann **gehen wir** …*

The last column, the Satzende, stays empty in this unit. It fills up in A1-U03 (separable verbs) and A1-U04 (modal verbs). More: [the Sentence Map](../Resources/Sentence_Map.md).

### 💬 Beispiele

- **Seit Mai wohnen** wir in Köln. → *We've been living in Cologne since May.*
- **Deutsch lerne** ich für meinen Job. → *I'm learning German for my job.* (German first to stress it)
- **Abends kocht** mein Mann. → *My husband cooks in the evenings.*
- **Am Wochenende besuchen** wir oft Freunde. → *At the weekend we often visit friends.*
- **Jetzt habe** ich leider keine Zeit. → *I'm afraid I don't have time right now.*

## §C — Asking questions `A1-U01-§C`

### 📘 Refresh

**Two kinds of question**, both on the Sentence Map:

| Vorfeld | Pos. 2 (Verb 1) | Mittelfeld | Satzende (Verb 2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Woher | kommst | du? | |
| *(empty)* | Kommst | du aus Spanien? | |

- **Question-word questions:** the question word goes first, then the verb. The voice usually **falls** at the end.
- **Yes/no questions:** the verb goes **first**. The voice **rises** at the end.
- **No "do"!** *Do you work?* → *Arbeitest du?*

**Question words:**

| Word | Meaning | Word | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **wer?** | who? ⚠️ | **wann?** | when? |
| **was?** | what? | **wie?** | how? |
| **wo?** | where? ⚠️ | **wie viel(e)?** | how much / many? |
| **woher?** | where from? | **warum?** | why? |
| **wohin?** | where to? | **welche(r/s)?** | which? |

⚠️ ***wer*** = who and ***wo*** = where. English makes you want to swap them. Make flashcards for these two.

**du or Sie?**

| | Use it with … | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| ***du*** | friends, family, children, and usually other students and young colleagues | *Wie heißt du? Woher kommst du?* |
| ***Sie*** | adults you don't know, officials, shop staff, older neighbours | *Wie heißen Sie? Woher kommen Sie?* |

If unsure, start with *Sie*; the other person will offer *du* if they want it.

**Follow-up questions** turn an interview into a conversation. After an answer, add one:
- *Und du? / Und Sie?*
- *Seit wann? / Wie lange schon?*
- *Wirklich?*
- *Wie findest du …?*
- *Warum?*
- *Wo genau?*

More in [Redemittel → Keeping a conversation going](../Resources/Redemittel.md#a1-keeping-a-conversation-going).

### 💬 Beispiele

- **Wie heißt** du? (natural) · ⚠️ *Was ist dein Name?* is correct but sounds like a form or an interrogation. [uncommon]
- **Woher kommen** Sie? · **Wo kommst** du **her**? [spoken]
- **Was machst** du beruflich? / **Was bist** du von Beruf? → *What do you do (for a living)?*
- **Seit wann wohnst** du hier? → *How long have you been living here?* (German uses the present tense with *seit*!)
- **Hast** du Geschwister? – Ja, einen Bruder. – **Und wie alt ist** er?

## §D — Numbers and personal data `A1-U01-§D`

### 📘 Refresh

**How German builds numbers:**

| Numbers | Rule | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 13–19 | number + *-zehn* | *dreizehn, vierzehn*; watch *se**ch**zehn* and *sie**b**zehn* (letters drop out) |
| Tens | *-zig* | *zwanzig*, ***dreißig*** (with ß!), *vierzig, fünfzig, se**ch**zig, sie**b**zig, achtzig, neunzig* |
| 21–99 | **units first**, then *und*, then tens | *einundzwanzig* (21), *achtundfünfzig* (58) |
| Hundreds, thousands | written as one word | *(ein)hundertzwölf* (112), *zweitausendfünfhundert* (2,500) |

- ⚠️ **21–99 is backwards compared with English.** Practise until you *hear* 58 as "eight-and-fifty" without translating.
- **Years before 2000 in hundreds:** *neunzehnhundertachtundneunzig* (1998). **From 2000:** *zweitausendvierundzwanzig* (2024).
- **Phone numbers:** digit by digit, or in pairs: *null-eins-sieben-sechs … achtunddreißig …* On the phone many people say ***zwo*** instead of *zwei*, so it can't be confused with *drei*. [spoken]
- **Prices:** *3,50 €* → *drei Euro fünfzig*. Germans use a **comma** for decimals and a **point** for thousands: *1.250,00 €*.
- **Age:** *Ich bin vierunddreißig (Jahre alt).*

**The alphabet: letters that differ from English:**

| Letter | Said | Letter | Said |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | *ah* | V | *fau* |
| E | *eh* | W | *weh* |
| I | *ih* | Y | *üpsilon* |
| J | *jott* | Z | *tsett* |
| H | *hah* | ß | *eszett* |
| U | *uh* | Ä / Ö / Ü | *a-Umlaut* / *o-Umlaut* / *u-Umlaut* |

**Forms (Formulare):**
- *der Vorname* (first name)
- *der Nachname / Familienname* (surname)
- *die Adresse* = *die Straße* + *die Hausnummer*
- *die Postleitzahl (PLZ)* (postcode)
- *der Wohnort* (town)
- *die Telefonnummer*, *die E-Mail-Adresse*
- *das Geburtsdatum* (date of birth)
- *der Familienstand* (marital status): *ledig / verheiratet / geschieden* (single / married / divorced)

### 19. 🗣️ Speed introduction: 60 → 45 → 30 seconds (~10 min)

Introduce yourself three times, recording each round. Keep **all** the content and use less time each round:
- **Round 1:** 60 seconds
- **Round 2:** 45 seconds
- **Round 3:** 30 seconds

**Must include:**
- name
- where you're from
- where you live, and since when
- job or studies
- languages
- family
- one free-time activity
- one sentence that does **not** start with *ich*

Round 3 should sound smoother than round 1. If it doesn't, do all three rounds again tomorrow. Technique: [Retelling against the clock](../Resources/Speaking_Toolkit.md#retelling-against-the-clock-4-3-2).

### 21. 🎭 Role-play: find three things in common (~12 min)

At a language café you meet someone your age. Your goal is to **find three things you have in common**: a language, a hobby, a place, a job …

**Success criteria:**
- you ask at least **8 questions**
- you ask at least **3 follow-up questions**
- you use *du*
- you name the three things you have in common at the end: *Wir sprechen beide … / Wir … beide …*

```text
[ROLE-PLAY — copy this into your AI app, voice mode if possible]
You are Jonas (or Julia), 30, from Hamburg, at a language café in Leipzig. I am a German learner at level A1.
Scenario: we meet for the first time. My goal: find three things we have in common.
Invent your details: job, languages, hobbies, family, where you live. Make sure at least three
things could match an average adult learner (e.g. a hobby, a language, a city).
Rules:
- Speak only German, at A1 level, in short turns (1–2 sentences). Use "du".
- Stay in role. Answer my questions; ask me a question back sometimes.
- At some point, mention something surprising about yourself (e.g. you speak Japanese).
- Do NOT correct me during the conversation.
- After 15 turns, or when I say "Ende", step out of the role and give me:
  1. up to 5 of my errors, each with the correction,
  2. 3 more natural phrases I could have used,
  3. one thing I did well.
Start now: "Hallo! Ist hier noch frei?"
```

No AI app? Write the dialogue for both people instead, then read both roles aloud.

## 👄 Aussprache-Minute: long and short vowels, ü and ö

1. Let a TTS voice read each pair, then **shadow** it.
   - **long – short:** *wohnen – kommen* · *Name – Stadt* · *wir – bin* · *Sohn – Sonne* · *leben – lernen*
   - **ü and ö:** *fünf · zwölf · München · Türkei · Köln · Österreich · hören · Brötchen*
2. Say this sentence three times and record the last version. Check your ü, ö and long vowels:

   > ***Fünf** **Schüler** aus **Köln** **wohnen** seit **zwölf** Jahren in **München**.*

Tip for **ü**: say *ie* (as in *vier*), then round your lips without moving your tongue. More in the [Pronunciation Guide](../Resources/Pronunciation_Guide.md#umlauts-ä-ö-ü).

## ⚠️ Typische Fehler

| ❌ Typical mistake | ✅ Correct | Why | Code |
|---|---|---|---|
| Du sprechst Deutsch. | Du **sprichst** Deutsch. | e → i for *du* and *er/sie/es* | VF |
| Heute ich arbeite. | Heute **arbeite ich**. | Verb in position 2 | WO |
| Ich komme **von** Kanada. | Ich komme **aus** Kanada. | Origin = *aus* | PR |
| Ich bin gut. (*I'm fine*) | **Mir geht's gut.** / Gut, danke. | *Ich bin gut* means "I'm good (at something)" | WC |
| **Wo** ist das? (meaning *who*) | **Wer** ist das? | *wer* = who, *wo* = where | WC |
| Ich habe hier zwei Jahre gewohnt. (*I've lived here for two years*, and still do) | Ich **wohne seit** zwei Jahren hier. | Still true → *seit* + present tense | VF |
| Ich bin **eine** Lehrerin. | Ich bin Lehrerin. | No article with jobs after *sein* | GE |
| Ich bin 30 Jahre. | Ich bin 30 (Jahre **alt**). | *Jahre* without *alt* sounds incomplete | WC |
| zwanzigeins · dreizig | **einundzwanzig** · **dreißig** | Units first; *dreißig* with ß | SP |
| Ihr seit neu hier. | Ihr **seid** neu hier. | *seid* = are; *seit* = since | SP |

Recognise one of your own mistakes here? Add it to your [Error Log](../Learner_Workbook/Error_Log.md).

**Countries and languages:**
- *Deutschland – Deutsch* · *Österreich* · *die Schweiz* (**aus der** Schweiz)
- *England / Großbritannien – Englisch* · *die USA* (**aus den** USA)
- *Spanien – Spanisch* · *Frankreich – Französisch* · *Italien – Italienisch* · *Polen – Polnisch*

Countries with an article (*die Schweiz, die Türkei, die USA*) take *der/den* after *aus*. For now, learn these as fixed phrases; A2-U02 explains why.
