# Speaking Toolkit

This course is built around one problem: you **understand** far more German than you can **say**. Understanding is recognition; speaking is retrieval under time pressure, and it is a different skill that has to be trained on purpose. This toolkit explains the techniques the lessons use, so you can also use them on your own. Lessons link to the sections below.

## The rules of speaking practice

1. **Out loud, always.** Saying a sentence in your head is not the same as saying it. Your mouth has to learn it too.
2. **Speed before perfection.** In a fast-answer drill, a quick answer with a small mistake is worth more than a perfect answer after 10 seconds. Accuracy comes from repetition; speed only comes from practising speed.
3. **Short and often.** Five minutes a day beats one hour a week.
4. **Retrieve first, then check.** Try to say it, then look. Never read the answer first "just to see".
5. **Repeat the same task on different days.** The second and third time are where fluency grows.
6. **Record regularly.** You can't hear yourself properly while speaking.

## Fast-answer drill

**Used in every A1 unit and many later lessons.**

1. Take a list of prompts: English sentences, situations, or questions.
2. Start a recording. For each prompt, **say the German answer immediately** and move on after about 8 seconds at the latest.
3. Listen back and mark each item ✓ fast / ~ slow / ✗ fail (the [fluency scale](Rubrics.md#fluency-scale)).
4. Repeat **only the ~ and ✗ items** until they are ✓. Then repeat the whole list on another day.

**Goal:** 80% ✓ at the stage level, which means you can use it in real conversation.

## Shadowing

**For:** pronunciation, rhythm, and the "feel" of German sentences in your mouth.

1. Take a short audio, 20–60 seconds: a lesson dialogue read by TTS, or any clear recording.
2. **Listen once** with the transcript.
3. **Speak along**, a split second behind the voice, like an echo. Copy the melody and speed, not just the words.
4. Do it 3–5 times until you can keep up without the transcript.
5. Record your final version and compare it with the original.

Start with slow TTS voices, then use normal speed.

## Retelling against the clock (4-3-2)

**For:** fluency. You tell the same content three times, with less time each round. The pressure forces you to retrieve faster and drop the pauses.

| Stage | Round 1 | Round 2 | Round 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | 60 s | 45 s | 30 s |
| A2 | 2 min | 1.5 min | 1 min |
| B1 | 4 min | 3 min | 2 min |

**How:** tell the same story or description three times, with all the same content, in less time each round. Record all three rounds. Round 3 should sound noticeably smoother than round 1. If it doesn't, do it again tomorrow.

## Self-talk

**For:** turning everyday life into speaking practice, for free.

- Describe what you are doing while you do it: *Jetzt koche ich Kaffee. Ich brauche Milch – oh, keine Milch mehr!*
- On your way home, tell yourself your day, then your plans for tomorrow.
- **Collect the gaps.** Every time you don't know a word, note it (*"How do you say 'kettle'?"*) and look it up later. That list is gold: it holds exactly the words *you* need. Put them into your [Chunk Bank](../Learner_Workbook/Chunk_Bank.md).

## Recording and reviewing yourself

After a recorded task, listen once and answer these questions. You'll find it much easier than rating yourself while speaking.

- [ ] Did I **complete the task** (all points)?
- [ ] How long did I speak? Was that the target length?
- [ ] Where did I **pause** for more than 3 seconds? What word or structure was I looking for?
- [ ] Did the verb stand in the right place (position 2, end of clause)?
- [ ] Did I use at least 2 phrases or words from this unit?
- [ ] One pronunciation point to fix (use the [checklist](Rubrics.md#pronunciation-and-intelligibility)).

Write your 1–3 most important mistakes in your [Error Log](../Learner_Workbook/Error_Log.md). Then **record the task again**. The second version is where learning happens.

## Question generation

**For:** conversation skills. Most learners can answer questions but rarely ask them.

- **From an answer:** read a sentence and ask as many questions about it as you can. *Anna wohnt seit zwei Jahren in Köln. → Wer wohnt in Köln? Wo wohnt Anna? Seit wann wohnt sie da? Gefällt es ihr?*
- **Interview a person:** before a conversation (or for a person in a photo), prepare 10 questions, then ask them without reading.
- **Follow-up rule:** after every answer you get, ask one follow-up question (*Seit wann? Warum? Wie war das?*).

## Describing a photo

**For:** retrieval of everyday vocabulary. Use photos from your own phone: they are about *your* life, so the words stay.

A simple frame that grows with your level:

| Level | Frame |
|---|---|
| A1 | *Das ist … Hier sind … Das ist in … Ich sehe …* |
| A2 | *Auf dem Foto sieht man … Links / rechts / in der Mitte … Das war letzten Sommer … Wir haben …* |
| B1 | *Das Foto zeigt … Ich glaube, dass … Es sieht so aus, als ob … Für mich bedeutet das Foto …* |

Timed version: 30 seconds per photo, then move to the next photo without stopping.

## Say it three ways

**For:** flexibility, so that one missing word never blocks you.

Take one idea and express it three different ways:
- *Ich habe keine Zeit.*
- *Ich kann leider nicht.*
- *Da passt es mir nicht.*

The lessons use this for key functions: refusing, suggesting, asking for help, giving opinions.

## Talking around a missing word

**For:** the real-life moment when a word is missing, which happens at every level.

| Stage | Strategy | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Use a general word + pointing or context | *Das Ding da, bitte. – Das hier?* |
| A1 | Ask directly | *Wie sagt man "kettle" auf Deutsch?* |
| A2 | Describe with *für* or *zum* | *Das ist etwas für Wasser … zum Kochen.* |
| A2 | Use the opposite | *nicht teuer* (for *günstig*) |
| B1 | Relative clause | *Das ist ein Gerät, mit dem man Wasser heiß macht.* |
| B1 | Kind of thing | *Das ist eine Art Werkzeug, das …* |

The B1 strategies are taught in B1-U02. Until then, the first four are enough.

## Practising with an AI partner

An AI chat app, ideally in **voice mode**, is a patient, always-available conversation partner. Every lesson with a role-play gives you a ready-made prompt. Copy it into the app. This is the general version for free conversation:

```text
[CONVERSATION PARTNER — copy this into your AI app, voice mode if possible]
You are a friendly German speaker. I am a German learner at level {A1/A2/B1}.
Let's talk about: {topic}.
Rules:
- Speak only German, at {level} level, in short turns (1–3 sentences), a little slower than normal.
- Ask me questions and follow-up questions; keep the conversation going.
- If I use English, answer in German and help me say it in German.
- Do NOT correct me during the conversation.
- When I say "Ende", give me: up to 5 of my errors with corrections, 3 more natural phrases I could have used, and one thing I did well.
Start with a question.
```

**Correction modes:**

| You want … | Add this line to the prompt |
|---|---|
| no correction at all (pure fluency practice) | *Never correct me. Just keep the conversation going.* |
| delayed correction (the default) | *Correct me only at the end.* |
| instant correction (for a single structure you are drilling) | *After each of my answers, repeat my sentence correctly in one short line, then continue.* |

Delayed correction is the best default. Being corrected mid-sentence breaks your flow and trains you to hesitate.

## Practising with people

The AI is good for practice; people are what the practice is for. From A2 on, aim for at least one real conversation every 1–2 weeks:
- a **tandem partner** (you practise German, they practise English), via tandem apps or local language cafés
- a **tutor**, for regular structured practice with expert feedback
- **everyday situations**: shops, cafés, neighbours, when you have the chance

Before a session, prepare 3 things you want to talk about and 2 structures you want to use. Afterwards, note in your Error Log the phrases you missed.
