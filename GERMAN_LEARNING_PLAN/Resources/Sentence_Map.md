# The Sentence Map (Satzkarte)

German word order looks like a pile of rules: verb second, verb at the end, prefixes that jump away, *weil* that sends the verb away. It is actually **one system**. This page shows that system as a map with four fields. Every structure in the course (separable verbs, modal verbs, Perfekt, *weil*-clauses, relative clauses, passive, Konjunktiv II) is just **a new way of filling the same map**.

Whenever a lesson introduces a verb structure, it shows it in this exact table format. Come back here whenever word order feels confusing.

---

## The four fields

| Vorfeld | Pos. 2 (Verb 1) | Mittelfeld | Satzende (Verb 2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **one** element, any kind | the **conjugated verb**, always | everything else | the **second verb part**, if there is one |

- **Vorfeld** ("front field"): exactly **one** element. Often the subject, but it can be a time, a place, an object, or even a whole clause.
- **Position 2**: the **conjugated verb**, the part that changes with the person (*arbeite, arbeitest, arbeitet* …). In a statement it is always here. That's the famous "verb second" rule.
- **Mittelfeld** ("middle field"): all the other information: subject (if not in front), objects, time, place, *nicht* …
- **Satzende** ("sentence end"): the **second part of the verb**: a separable prefix, an infinitive, or a participle. It closes the sentence.

Position 2 and the Satzende together form the **Satzklammer** (sentence bracket): the verb opens the sentence and closes it, and the information sits in between.

---

## 1. Statements: the verb sits in position 2

| Vorfeld | Pos. 2 (Verb 1) | Mittelfeld | Satzende (Verb 2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ich | arbeite | heute zu Hause. | |
| Heute | arbeite | ich zu Hause. | |
| Zu Hause | arbeite | ich heute. | |

**The rule:** one element in front, then the verb. When something other than the subject goes in front, the subject moves **behind** the verb.

> ⚠️ **English interference.** English allows two things before the verb (*Today **I** work at home*). German doesn't:
> ❌ *Heute ich arbeite zu Hause.* → ✅ *Heute arbeite ich zu Hause.*

Why move things to the front? To connect to what was said before, or to set the scene. *Am Wochenende schlafe ich lange* sounds natural because the time sets the scene. Starting every sentence with *ich* is correct, but sounds monotonous.

## 2. Questions

| Vorfeld | Pos. 2 (Verb 1) | Mittelfeld | Satzende (Verb 2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wo | arbeitest | du? | |
| Wann | fängt | der Kurs | an? |
| *(empty)* | Arbeitest | du heute? | |
| *(empty)* | Kannst | du mir | helfen? |

- **Question-word questions:** the question word takes the Vorfeld, and the verb is still in position 2.
- **Yes/no questions:** the Vorfeld stays **empty**, so the verb comes first.
- No "do": ❌ *Machst du arbeiten?* → ✅ *Arbeitest du?* German just moves the verb.

## 3. The bracket: when the verb has two parts

| Vorfeld | Pos. 2 (Verb 1) | Mittelfeld | Satzende (Verb 2) | Structure | Taught in |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ich | rufe | dich morgen | an. | separable verb | [A1-U03](../A1/A1-U03_Mein_Tag.md) |
| Ich | muss | heute lange | arbeiten. | modal verb + infinitive | [A1-U04](../A1/A1-U04_Koennen_muessen_duerfen.md) |
| Ich | muss | dich morgen | anrufen. | modal + separable verb | A1-U04 |
| Ich | habe | gestern lange | gearbeitet. | Perfekt | [A2-U01](../A2/A2-U01_Erlebnisse/00_Overview_und_Wortschatz.md) |
| Ich | würde | gern nach Italien | fahren. | Konjunktiv II | [A2-U10](../A2/A2-U10_Medien_und_Technik/00_Overview_und_Wortschatz.md) |
| Es | wird | morgen | regnen. | future | [A2-U09](../A2/A2-U09_Feste_und_Plaene/00_Overview_und_Wortschatz.md) |
| Das Haus | wird | gerade | renoviert. | passive | [B1-U06](../B1/B1-U06_Medien_und_Nachrichten/00_Overview_und_Wortschatz.md) |

**The rule:** the conjugated part goes to position 2, the rest to the **very end**. Everything else goes in between, however long it gets:

> Ich muss … anrufen.
> Ich muss **heute Abend noch schnell meine Mutter** anrufen.

When a separable verb is used with a modal verb, the prefix and the verb join again at the end (*anrufen*). When a separable verb is the only verb, the prefix goes to the end alone (*rufe … an*).

> ⚠️ **English interference.** English keeps the verb parts together (*I have **worked** a lot today*). German spreads them out: *Ich **habe** heute viel **gearbeitet**.* Say the bracket in your head: *habe … gearbeitet*.

## 4. Verb-final clauses: the verb's seat is taken

| Vorfeld | Pos. 2 (Verb 1) | Mittelfeld | Satzende (Verb 2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| *(empty)* | weil | ich meine Mutter | anrufen muss. |
| *(empty)* | dass | der Kurs morgen | anfängt. |
| *(empty)* | wenn | du Zeit | hast. |
| *(empty)* | ob | er heute | kommen kann. |

This is the most useful idea on this page. In a subordinate clause, a conjunction (*weil, dass, wenn, ob, als* …) **takes the Position-2 seat**. The verb has nowhere to go, so the **whole verb goes to the end**, with the conjugated part last: *anrufen **muss***, *angerufen **hat***.

- A separable verb joins up again at the end: ✅ *…, dass der Kurs morgen **anfängt**.*
- Relative clauses work the same way. The relative pronoun takes the seat: *der Mann, **der** dort **wohnt*** ([B1-U02](../B1/B1-U02_Menschen_beschreiben/00_Overview_und_Wortschatz.md)).
- So do indirect questions: *Weißt du, **wo** der Bahnhof **ist**?* ([A2-U07](../A2/A2-U07_Reisen_und_Verkehr/00_Overview_und_Wortschatz.md)).

**When the subordinate clause comes first**, the whole clause *is* the Vorfeld. The main verb follows immediately, so you get the pattern **verb, comma, verb**:

| Vorfeld | Pos. 2 (Verb 1) | Mittelfeld | Satzende (Verb 2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weil ich krank bin, | bleibe | ich heute zu Hause. | |
| Wenn du Zeit hast, | kannst | du mich | anrufen. |

> 💬 In casual **speech**, many Germans say *weil ich hab keine Zeit*, with the verb in position 2. It's common and accepted in speech, but wrong in writing. Learn the verb-final version first. [spoken]

## 5. Position 0: *und, aber, oder, denn, sondern*

These five words **don't count** in the map. They stand in front of it, in "position 0", and join two complete main clauses:

| Pos. 0 | Vorfeld | Pos. 2 | Mittelfeld | Satzende |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| …, aber | heute | arbeite | ich zu Hause. | |
| …, denn | ich | bin | krank. | |
| …, und | dann | gehen | wir ins Kino. | |

Compare *denn* and *weil*. They mean nearly the same, but *denn* keeps the verb in position 2 and *weil* sends it to the end:
- *Ich bleibe zu Hause, **denn** ich **bin** krank.*
- *Ich bleibe zu Hause, **weil** ich krank **bin**.*

## 6. Inside the Mittelfeld

The Mittelfeld has tendencies rather than strict rules. Four are worth knowing early:

1. **Short pronouns come early:** *Ich gebe **es ihm** morgen.* / *Kannst du **mir** helfen?*
2. **Time before place:** *Ich fahre **morgen nach Berlin**.* The fuller order is time – reason – manner – place: *Ich fahre morgen wegen der Arbeit mit dem Zug nach Berlin* (built up in A2 and B1).
3. ***nicht*** goes before the part it negates, or late in the Mittelfeld when it negates the whole sentence: *Ich kann heute **nicht** kommen.* / *Ich finde den Film **nicht** gut.* ([A1-U04](../A1/A1-U04_Koennen_muessen_duerfen.md))
4. **The subject pronoun sits right after the verb** when something else is in front: *Heute **arbeite ich** …*, not *Heute arbeite zu Hause ich.*

## 7. Using the map when you speak

- **Choose the verb first.** Before you start a sentence, know which verb you need and whether it has a second part. Then park the second part in your mind and say the middle.
- **Front-field drill.** Take one sentence and say it 3 times with a different element in front: *Ich trinke abends Tee. → Abends trinke ich Tee. → Tee trinke ich abends* (for contrast).
- **Bracket-stretch drill.** Start with a short bracket and make the middle longer each time, without losing the end: *Ich muss anrufen. → Ich muss dich anrufen. → Ich muss dich heute noch anrufen. → Ich muss dich heute Abend noch schnell anrufen.*
- **Verb-end drill.** Turn statements into *weil*-, *dass*- and *wenn*-clauses as fast as you can: *Ich bin müde. → …, weil ich müde bin.*

These drills appear throughout the lessons. Doing them aloud, against a timer, is what turns the map from knowledge into a reflex.
