## How to rate

1. Rate **each criterion separately** from 0 to 4. Half points (e.g. 2.5) are fine.
2. The **overall score** is the average of the five criteria, rounded to the nearest 0.5.
3. Always rate **against the level of the task**, not against a native speaker. A "4" in an A1 task means "excellent *for this A1 task*".
4. Rating yourself is hard. Most learners are too harsh on pronunciation and too generous on accuracy. Get a second opinion when you can:
   - an AI chat with the prompts at the end of this page
   - a tandem partner
   - a tutor

   If two ratings differ by more than 1 point on a criterion, look at that criterion again together.
5. A rating is only useful if you **act on it**. After every rated task, write your 1–3 most important mistakes in your Error Log.

## Speaking rubric

| Score | Task completion | Range (words & structures) | Accuracy | Fluency & coherence | Pronunciation & intelligibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **4** | Everything the task asks for; right length; purpose clearly achieved; answers follow-up questions | Varied vocabulary and structures, some above the stage minimum; natural chunks | Few errors, none that confuse; basic structures (verb forms, verb position) consistently correct | Speaks with ease for the stage; pauses only to think about content; ideas clearly linked | Easy to understand; key sounds and word stress mostly right |
| **3** | Most content points; purpose achieved | Adequate for the task; some variety | Some errors, but meaning always clear; basic structures mostly correct | Some hesitation, but mostly continuous; simple linking | Understood with little effort; some sound errors |
| **2** | Some points missing, or clearly too short; purpose partly achieved | Limited; repeats the same words; gaps force simplification | Frequent errors; meaning sometimes unclear; basic structures unstable | Frequent pauses and restarts; a list of sentences rather than a text | Listener needs effort; several words misunderstood |
| **1** | Very little relevant content | Isolated words or memorised phrases only | Errors often block understanding | Very halting; long silences; mostly single words | Often unintelligible |
| **0** | Nothing relevant | – | – | Cannot sustain speech | Unintelligible |

## Writing rubric

| Score | Task completion | Range | Accuracy | Coherence | Organisation & conventions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **4** | All content points; length within ±20%; the right text type | Varied, precise, some above the stage minimum; natural collocations | Few errors, none that confuse | Ideas in a logical order, well linked; paragraphs where appropriate | Correct greeting and closing; consistent *du* or *Sie*; commas before *weil/dass/wenn*; clean layout |
| **3** | Most points; length roughly right | Adequate, some variety | Some errors; meaning always clear | Mostly logical order; simple linking | Mostly correct; small slips in register or punctuation |
| **2** | Some points missing, or much too short | Limited; repetitive | Frequent errors; sometimes unclear | Jumps between ideas; little linking | Missing greeting or closing, or mixed *du/Sie* |
| **1** | Very little relevant content | Isolated words and phrases | Errors often block understanding | No visible structure | Conventions largely missing |
| **0** | Nothing relevant | – | – | – | – |

## Stage anchors: what a 3 looks like

| | A1 | A2 | B1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Speaking: length** | 2–4 connected sentences (20–30 s) | 30–60 s of connected speech | 2–3 min, with an opening, main points and a conclusion |
| **Speaking: structures** | Present tense and verb-second order mostly right; simple questions | Perfekt for the past; *weil*, *denn*, *und*, *aber*, *dann*; a few dative phrases | Subordinate clauses; opinions with reasons and counter-arguments; handles follow-up questions; talks around missing words |
| **Writing: length** | 20–30 words | 50–80 words | 80–120 words |
| **Writing: typical text** | Note, form, short message | Personal email, description, short story in Perfekt | Forum post, formal email, complaint, story, summary |
| **Writing: linking** | *und*, *aber* | *dann*, *danach*, *weil*, *deshalb*, *trotzdem* | *obwohl*, *außerdem*, *einerseits … andererseits*, *deshalb*, *zum Schluss* |

## Fluency scale

Used for timed retrieval tasks (e.g. Diagnostic Part 6 and the "say it in German" drills in lessons). Record yourself and mark each item:

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ✓ **fast** | You started within about **3 seconds** and the sentence is correct (a tiny slip is OK) |
| ~ **slow** | Correct, but you needed more than 3 seconds or restarted |
| ✗ **fail** | Wrong, or nothing usable within about 8 seconds |

| Share of ✓ | Band | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 80–100% | **Automatic** | You can use this in real conversation |
| 55–79% | **Developing** | You know it but still build it; repeat the timed drill on another day |
| 0–54% | **Slow** | Retrieval is the priority: do more short, timed repetitions, not more explanation |

## Pronunciation and intelligibility

### Checklist

Compare your recording with a TTS voice reading the same text. Tick the features that are clearly different from the model. Each feature points to the part of the course that trains it.

| Feature | Examples | Trained in |
|---|---|---|
| Long vs short vowels | *Käse* (long) – *Milch* (short); *Sohn* – *Sonne* | A1-U01 |
| Umlauts ä, ö, ü | *Jürgen, Zürich, Brötchen, fährt* | A1-U01 |
| Final devoicing | *Tag* → [k], *und* → [t], *Rad* → [t] | A1-U02 |
| *ch* after e/i/ä/ö/ü (soft) and after a/o/u (hard) | *ich, Milch* – *kochen, auch* | A1-U02 |
| German *r*, including vowel-like *-er* | *Regen, Karten, über, Lehrer* | A1-U03 |
| *ei / ie / eu* | *einer – spielen – heute* | A1-U03 |
| *s, z, sch, sp, st* | *Sonne, Zürich, zusammen, spielen, Stadt* | A1-U04 |
| Stress in compound words | *BAHNhof, FAHRkarte, WOchenende* | A1-U05 |
| Glottal stop before vowels | *am | Abend*, *ein | Ei* (no linking as in English) | A2 |
| Prefix stress | *ANrufen* vs. *beSUCHen* | A2 |
| Sentence rhythm and melody | Questions rise or fall; stress on the new information | B1 |

### Dictation test bands

Read test sentences into phone dictation set to German. Count the words transcribed correctly.

| Share correct | Result | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 90–100% | Clearly intelligible | Keep the pronunciation minutes short |
| 75–89% | Mostly intelligible | Work on the checklist features you ticked |
| < 75% | Intelligibility is a priority | Do every pronunciation minute aloud twice; shadow daily (see the Speaking Toolkit) |

The dictation test is a rough proxy: software isn't a person, and room noise and microphones matter. Always use the same device, so results are comparable.

## Error categories

Use these codes in your [Error Log](../Learner_Workbook/Error_Log.md) and when you ask an AI for feedback. Using the same codes everywhere lets you see which *types* of mistakes keep coming back.

| Code | Category | Example (❌ → ✅) |
|---|---|---|
| **WO** | Word order | ❌ *Morgen ich arbeite.* → ✅ *Morgen arbeite ich.* |
| **VF** | Verb form | ❌ *Du sprechst* → ✅ *Du sprichst* |
| **CA** | Case | ❌ *Ich sehe der Mann.* → ✅ *Ich sehe den Mann.* |
| **GE** | Gender / article | ❌ *der Zeitung* → ✅ *die Zeitung* |
| **AE** | Adjective ending | ❌ *ein neue Auto* → ✅ *ein neues Auto* |
| **PR** | Preposition | ❌ *warten für* → ✅ *warten auf* |
| **WC** | Word choice / collocation | ❌ *eine Entscheidung machen* → ✅ *eine Entscheidung treffen* |
| **SP** | Spelling / capitalisation | ❌ *das haus* → ✅ *das Haus* |
| **PU** | Punctuation | ❌ *Ich glaube dass …* → ✅ *Ich glaube, dass …* |
| **RE** | Register (*du/Sie*, too formal or informal) | ❌ *Hallo Herr Weber, wie geht's dir?* → ✅ *Guten Tag, Herr Weber, wie geht es Ihnen?* |
| **PRN** | Pronunciation | *Käse* said with a short vowel; *ich* said like *isch* |

## AI rating prompts

Copy a prompt into an AI chat app, fill in the `{…}` parts, and paste your text or transcript underneath. AI ratings are a helpful second opinion, not a verdict. When the AI and you disagree strongly, check the rubric descriptions yourself.

### Rating a written text

```text
You are a fair and precise German teacher. Rate my text at CEFR level {A1/A2/B1}.
Task: {paste the task}
Use exactly these five criteria, each 0–4 (half points allowed), rating against the level of the task:
Task completion · Range · Accuracy · Coherence · Organisation & conventions.
Then:
1. List up to 8 errors. For each: my version → correct version → category code
   (WO word order, VF verb form, CA case, GE gender, AE adjective ending, PR preposition,
   WC word choice, SP spelling, PU punctuation, RE register).
2. Give a corrected version that keeps my ideas and stays at level {level}. Don't make it more advanced.
3. Suggest 3 more natural phrases I could have used.
4. Tell me the ONE thing I should work on next.
My text:
{paste your text}
```

### Rating a spoken answer (from a transcript)

To get a transcript, read your recording back into phone dictation, or use an app that transcribes audio. A transcript hides pronunciation problems, so rate pronunciation yourself with the checklist above.

```text
You are a fair German teacher. Below is a transcript of my spoken answer (CEFR level {A1/A2/B1}).
Task: {paste the task}. I spoke for {n} seconds.
Rate these four criteria, each 0–4, against the level of the task:
Task completion · Range · Accuracy · Fluency & coherence (judge linking and structure; the pauses are not visible in the transcript).
Then list up to 6 errors (my version → correction → category code: WO, VF, CA, GE, AE, PR, WC, RE),
give 3 natural phrases I could use next time, and name the ONE thing to work on next.
Transcript:
{paste}
```

## Thresholds used in checkpoints

These come from the course design and are repeated here for convenience:

| Checkpoint | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **A1 Checkpoint** | ≥ 75% in the written and listening parts; speaking average ≥ 2.5; fluency drill "Developing" or better |
| **A2 Exit** | ≥ 70% in reading and listening; task completion ≥ 3 in both speaking and writing |
| **B1 Exit** | ≥ 70% in each skill |

The official B1 exams pass at 60%. The course sets a higher bar on purpose, so your level holds up in real life.
